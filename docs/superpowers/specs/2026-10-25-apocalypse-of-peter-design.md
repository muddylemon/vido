# The Apocalypse of Peter (Thiel) — design spec

A ~2:10 animated film: a sixteenth-century apocalypse book whose woodcuts come alive.
Source: David Gibson, "The Gospel according to Peter (Thiel)," *The Verge*, 20 Oct 2026, an analysis
of four leaked Thiel lectures on the Antichrist (San Francisco 2025, Rome March 2026) and his
*First Things* essay "The Pope and the Antichrist" (July 2026).

## 1. Premise and form

Apocalyptic literature has a grammar: a seer, a locked place of revelation, "and I saw," numbered
sequences (seven seals, seven trumpets), composite beasts made of symbolic parts, an angel who
explains, a sorting of the saved from the damned, and the formula "here is wisdom." The film uses
that grammar to animate Thiel's theology on its own terms. The irony belongs to the genre, not to
the narrator: the last leaf does what every apocalypse does (sort the world), and the seer's mirror
returns the number to him. The film never says Thiel is wrong; the form says it.

Visual model: Dürer's *Apocalypse* (1498) and Cranach's Protestant broadsheet *Passional Christi
und Antichristi* (1521), the woodcut pairing of Christ and the Antichrist-pope. The film is an
**open two-page spread**: verse letterpressed on the left (verso), woodcut panel on the right
(recto). Between leaves the right page turns. The woodcut figures move with the stiff, hinged
"cutout puppet" motion the project's engine already produces.

Formal arc (from Ratzinger's line, which Thiel quotes): *apocalyptic images are like a torrent of
water that overflows the banks*. The woodcut's frame is the bank. Leaf by leaf the images break the
frame a little more (a spurt of water in II, eyes in the text margins in IV) until in VII the ink
floods both pages and only HERE IS WISDOM remains.

Title: **THE APOCALYPSE OF PETER (Thiel) — A Woodcut in Seven Leaves.** (The apocryphal 2nd-century
*Apocalypse of Peter* nearly entered the canon; the parenthesis echoes the Verge headline.)

No narration. The verse is the voice; the score carries the trumpets, bells, and drone. Text is
revealed word by word as if being inked, then held long enough to read.

## 2. The script

Verses are in KJV cadence. **Rubrics (¶, vermilion italic) are verbatim Thiel quotations from the
article**; the colophon's quotation is Ratzinger's as quoted by Thiel. Verse numbers are vermilion
superscripts. Timing is global seconds; each leaf's right page turns in during its first 0.7 s.

### Leaf 0 — Title · 0:00–0:11
Left page:
> THE APOCALYPSE OF PETER
> (Thiel)
> A Woodcut in Seven Leaves
>
> Which was shewn him in a locked room in San Francisco, and again at the gates of Rome; that all might hear of it, and none be let in.
>
> ¶ "It's a pretty good marketing shtick if you want everybody to hear about something, not to let anybody in the room."

Woodcut: a heavy door with a keyhole, through which a lectern lamp glows. Woodcut ears and
profiles press against the door from outside. At 2.0 s the bolt slides home (hinged snap, CLACK).
Light from the keyhole flickers on the listeners' faces. The seer is never shown in full here:
only a hooded silhouette at the lectern through the keyhole.

### Leaf I — Of the Four Quadrants · 0:11–0:26
> ¹And I saw the age to come divided as a field, into four parts. ²In the first, unjust war. In the second, just war. In the third, a just peace. ³And in the fourth was peace, and it was unjust; and the riders slept in their saddles. ⁴And a voice said: Put odds on it. Are we not trending towards the fourth quadrant?
>
> ¶ "We're all scared to death that we're sleepwalking into Armageddon."

Woodcut: the panel is inked as a 2×2 matrix (a whiteboard quadrant, in woodcut), the lines drawn
on at 11.5–13 s with labels UNJUST WAR · JUST WAR · JUST PEACE · UNJUST PEACE. Four horsemen of the
Apocalypse ride in, one per quadrant (hoofbeats): sword raised, sword sheathed, dove, pillow. At
~19 s the fourth rider's head drops (snap) and the horse kneels; a lapis tide of ink rises in the
fourth quadrant like a blanket, and a banner unfurls across it: PEACE AND SAFETY · 1 Thess. 5:3.
The tide laps over the panel's bottom rule (first small overflow).

### Leaf II — Of the Restrainer · 0:26–0:40
> ¹And I saw one that restraineth; and his name in the Greek is Katechon, because of course it is. ²He stood in the breach with a mask upon his face and a clipboard in his hand, and held back a torrent; and the torrent was the future. ³And behind him the people slept under a banner; and the banner said, PEACE AND SAFETY.
>
> ¶ "If you're too troubled, will we always end up with very unjust peace?"

Woodcut: a giant robed figure in a surgical mask, arms spread, feet braced, clipboard in one
hand, holding back an engraved torrent (Hokusai/Dürer waves) that pours from the right. In the
water tumble the Baconian future: rockets, gears, a retort, an atom, a lightbulb. Behind him,
little sleepers in rows under the lapis PEACE AND SAFETY banner. The torrent pushes (he slides,
re-braces with snaps). At ~36 s a jet spurts past his elbow, over the frame rule, and runs down
the page margin (overflow).

### Leaf III — Of the Seven Trumpets · 0:40–1:04
> ¹And seven angels sounded seven trumpets; and it was told me, Even a Mozart cannot make a trumpet forget to sound like a trumpet. ²And at each trumpet a face was shewn me, and I asked: Is it he?

Then a litany, one line appearing per trumpet (every ~2.7 s from 44 s), with the rubric changing:

| # | Litany line (left page) | Rubric | Medallion (right page) |
|---|---|---|---|
| i | A man with two faces, who would give his soul for a prize of peace. | "a Dr. Jekyll and Mr. Hyde-type character" | Janus head: cardigan-and-smile half, snarling half; a peace medal on a ribbon |
| ii | The shadow of a girl with a sign; a type, and not the thing itself. | "I don't want to flatter her too much." | An empty medallion; only a cast shadow of a girl holding a placard |
| iii | A black pill. | "more depressed and more Luddite than Greta" | A single black pill, gleaming, on a cushion |
| iv | A face, crossed out; for it was not popular. | "the Antichrist is popular." | A smiling bald head, then a vermilion X stamped over it |
| v | Two crossed keys; and the voice said, He could have been. | "could have been" | Crossed keys of St Peter, one key broken |
| vi | A triple crown fused to a dome of state, and upon it a red star. | "a woke American pope" | Papal tiara grown into a Capitol dome; a vermilion star on top |
| vii | And at the seventh trumpet no face was shewn me, but a machinery. | — | The medallion fills with gear-teeth and a single eye opening |

Each trumpet: an angel's horn pokes in from a corner of the panel, blast, the medallion swings in on
a hinge (snap). Faces are emblematic woodcut types, not portraits.

### Leaf IV — Of the Beast that is a Machinery · 1:04–1:20
> ¹And I beheld a beast that was not a man, but a machinery; built not overnight, but relatively quickly, under cover of peace and safety. ²It was made of tax treaties and sanctions and the surveillance of money, and it was full of eyes before and behind; ³that no man might hide his money. ⁴And there was a switch.
>
> ¶ "There's a feeling it could be activated at the flip of a switch."

Woodcut: the beast assembles itself from cutouts snapping into place: a body of stacked ledgers,
gear-teeth jaws, filing-drawer ribs, paper-tape tentacles stamped TREATY · SANCTION · SURVEILLANCE,
and eyes in every rivet (Rev. 4:6). Eyes blink and swivel. A small man in a frock coat tiptoes
across the foreground with a sack of gold; every eye tracks him; a tape wraps his ankle. At right a
great knife-switch, OFF. A gloved hand descends from the top of the panel and rests on the lever
(72–78 s); the whole panel trembles. Overflow: eyes open in the left page's margins, watching the
verse.

### Leaf V — Of the Age of the Beast · 1:20–1:30
> ¹And it was told me, the Antichrist must make his mark quickly; more quickly than the Christ, who died at thirty-three and some months. ²And I said, But we are a gerontocracy. ³And the voice said: Then sixty-six is the new thirty-three.
>
> ¶ "in our gerontocracy, 66 is the new 33."

Woodcut: an hourglass between two roundels. Left roundel: a young face with 33½ on its brow. Sand
runs (tick-tock). At ~86 s the face ages in two snaps (beard, white hair, spectacles) and the brow
number flips like a split-flap board: 33½ → 66. A tiny "the new" is inked between.

### Leaf VI — Of the Wink · 1:30–1:43
> ¹And I saw a pope lay down his crown and go into a quiet room, and say nothing; and I understood that his silence was a trumpet, and his resignation was a wink. ²For had he spoken one peep of it, it would have dominated everything. ³And he left breadcrumbs; and I got down on my knees, and gathered them.
>
> ¶ "these apocalyptic paroxysms make Benedict sound like a fire-and-brimstone Southern Baptist."

Woodcut: a white-robed pope (emblematic, no portrait) sets the triple crown on the floor (thud),
walks stiffly to a small door, turns, and WINKS (one eyelid snaps shut, 96 s, a tiny bell). From
his sleeve falls a dotted trail of breadcrumbs. The hooded seer crawls in on hands and knees after
him, gathering crumbs into his hood. Door closes.

### Leaf VII — Here is Wisdom · 1:43–2:02
> ¹And when the trumpets were done, the seer went up to the seat of judgment, as every seer hath done, and divided the world. ²And the saved were few, and were in the room; and the damned were many, and were outside the door. ³And he lifted a glass to see the face of the Antichrist; and the glass shewed him a face, and upon its forehead a number; and the number was his own.
>
> HERE IS WISDOM.

Woodcut: the lectern of Leaf 0 has become a throne; the locked door stands centre. The hooded seer
sweeps little figures with a staff: gold ones through the door (few), vermilion ones out past the
frame (many). At ~113 s he raises a hand-mirror; the panel zooms into the glass: his hooded face,
and on the brow the number 66 inked in vermilion; around the mirror's rim the machinery's eyes
open one by one. At ~117 s the frame rule breaks and ink floods both pages (torrent roar); the
flood goes black; HERE IS WISDOM is knocked out in vellum, centred, alone (118–122 s).

### Colophon · 2:02–2:10
On bare vellum, small:
> Set from four lectures leaked to The Verge, given at San Francisco and Rome, MMXXV–MMXXVI; and from "The Pope and the Antichrist," First Things, July MMXXVI. After David Gibson.
>
> ¶ "Apocalyptic images are like a torrent of water that overflows the banks, and it is difficult to keep them in bounds." — Ratzinger, quoting another professor; quoted by Thiel.

A printer's device beneath: a small woodcut door with a keyhole. Fade to the desk.

## 3. Visual language (style guide)

- Canvas 1280×720, 30 fps, DURATION 130 s. Deterministic: everything is a function of `t`.
- The desk: near-black warm cloth `#231a14` with faint texture and a candle vignette (warm centre,
  dark corners, slow flicker ±3%).
- The spread: left page x 40–636, right page x 644–1240, y 24–696; gutter shadow at x 640. Page
  colour vellum `#e8dcc0`, shaded to `#d9cba8` toward the gutter; paper grain multiplied on.
- Text block (left page): x 92–588. Running head at y 72 (letter-spaced small caps, vermilion,
  18px: "THE FIRST LEAF · OF THE FOUR QUADRANTS"). Verse from y 112, Palatino Linotype 25px,
  line-height 34px, lampblack; verse numbers vermilion 15px superscript. Woodcut drop cap for the
  first letter (3 lines tall, letter in a hatched box with a vine). Rubric at the bottom of the
  block, Palatino italic 21px vermilion, prefixed ¶. Litany lines (Leaf III) are 23px.
- Word reveal: each word fades in over 0.18 s with a slight ink bloom (drawn at 1.15 scale and
  alpha 0.35 for the first frames), paced so the verse completes at 60% of the leaf; then held.
- Woodcut panel (right page): outer double rule at x 684–1200, y 64–656 (lampblack 3px + 1px);
  art clipped to the inner rect unless the leaf's overflow is active. Figures are built with the
  cutout engine (`cutout`, `ink`, `hatch`, `stipple`) in **lampblack line only**, with hatching for
  tone. Colour is spot-colour: vermilion `#b8321f` (rubrication, blood, the mark, the X, the star),
  gold leaf `#c9a13b`/`#efd98a` (crowns, coins, the saved, the medal), lapis `#2c4f7c` (PEACE AND
  SAFETY, the tide, the banner, the beast's tapes). Nothing else. Line weight 2.2 main, 0.9 hatch.
- Misregistration: the vermilion and lapis layers print 1.5px right and 1px down from the black.
  Implemented in post as a per-frame channel offset of a colour-keyed layer (book.js handles it:
  leaves draw colour normally and post offsets it).
- Motion: hinged snaps (`snap`), 15 fps stepping for figure motion (`stepT(t,15)`), no easing
  curves on figures except `eBack`; cameras ease. Things that live (eyes, water, flame) run at 30.
- The frame may be broken only by the overflow beats listed above; keep everything else inside.
- No portrait likenesses. Faces are woodcut types (the Janus head, the pope, the hooded seer).

## 4. Architecture

```
apocalypse/
  index.html        canvas #film 1280×720; loads the scripts below in order
  js/core.js        W,H,FPS,DURATION, PAL, RNG/seed, easings, snap/stepT, textures, cutout, spr,
                    engraving tools (copied from ../js/core.js and re-palleted; adds text helpers)
  js/book.js        the book: desk, pages, typesetting + word reveal, drop caps, rubrics, running
                    heads, litany, panel frame/clip, overflow flag, page turn, post-processing
  js/leaf0.js … leaf7.js, colophon.js   per leaf: buildLeafN() sprites into SP.lN; leafN(g, t)
  js/score.js       synthesized soundtrack (same API/IIFE shape as ../js/audio.js)
  js/main.js        TIMELINE table, drawAt(g,t) with page turns, renderAt(t) + post, ?t= / ?record
capture-server.js   generalized: /frame?i&name, /done?fps&name&dur&audio (defaults keep the old film)
```

Contracts:

- `TIMELINE = [{ id:'title', t0:0, t1:11, draw: leaf0 }, …]` in main.js. `drawAt(g,t)` picks the
  leaf; during the first 0.7 s of a leaf it draws the new spread, then the previous leaf's right
  page curling over it (`book.pageTurn(g, k, drawPrev)`).
- Each leaf: `function leafN(g, t)` draws the complete spread for global time `t` by calling
  `book.spread(g, t, SPEC_N, (g, p) => { …woodcut… })` where `SPEC_N` holds `{ head, verse, rubric,
  litany?, t0, t1, revealEnd, overflow(t) }` and `p` is the panel rect `{x,y,w,h}` with the clip
  already applied (unless `overflow(t)` is true, when the leaf draws unclipped and is responsible
  for staying tasteful). Sprites built in `buildLeafN()` go in `SP.lN` (e.g. `SP.l3.trumpet`).
  All globals are prefixed `lN_`.
- Post (book.js): paper grain multiply, misregistration of colour, candle flicker, vignette, a
  very light film grain, no projector weave (this is a book, not a film print).
- Score: `renderSoundtrack(SR, DUR)` returns `{L,R,sampleRate}`; cues are pinned to the timeline
  below. Browser plays it via AudioBuffer; Node writes WAV and muxes.
- Recording: `?record` posts PNG frames to the capture server with `name=apocalypse`; `/done`
  renders the score and encodes `out/apocalypse-of-peter.mp4`. Stills: `?t=<s>`; headless Edge
  (`msedge --headless=new --screenshot`) renders a still in ~4 s for verification.

## 5. Score cue sheet (seconds)

Key D minor, slow (60 bpm pulse where pulsed). Instruments: organ drone (D2+A2, swells at leaf
starts), church bell, trumpet (bright sawtooth with brass formant and 40 ms attack), choir aah
(existing additive formant voice), timpani, ratchet, tick-tock, paper swish for page turns, water.

| t | cue |
|---|---|
| 0–130 | room tone + candle crackle; organ drone fades in 0–3, out 126–130 |
| 2.0 | bolt CLACK (thud + metallic clink) |
| 11, 26, 40, 64, 80, 90, 103, 122 | page turn: paper swish 0.5 s + small bell on the new leaf |
| 12–17 | four horsemen enter: hoofbeat groups, a timpani stroke as each halts |
| 19.0 | the sleeper: descending organ sigh; 20–26 lapis tide: low filtered noise swell |
| 26–40 | torrent: water noise rising; 29, 33 restrainer re-braces (creaks); 32–38 choir aah |
| 36.0 | spurt over the frame: a thin whoosh |
| 44.0 + 2.7·i (i=0..6) | trumpet i: notes D4 F4 A4 D5 F5 A5, the seventh a cluster + ratchet clank |
| 64–80 | machinery: ratchet at 2 Hz, eye clicks, tape whirr; 72–78 silence but a heartbeat; 77.6 lever half-engages: soft clack |
| 80–90 | tick-tock; 86.0 split-flap snap + bell |
| 90–103 | single church bell every 4 s; 92 crown thud; 96 wink: ting + pizzicato; 97–102 crumb clicks |
| 103–113 | organ chorale, choir; small bell per sorted figure |
| 113–116 | silence (room tone) as the mirror rises |
| 116.5 | one trumpet, long, that will not forget itself; 117 flood roar 2 s |
| 118 | hard cut to room tone under HERE IS WISDOM |
| 122–130 | drone, fade |

## 6. Verification and tooling

- `npm run serve` starts capture-server.js (static files + frame sink, port 8765).
- `bash still.sh [-f film.html] [-p profile] <t>…` renders frame-exact stills through headless
  Edge (~4 s each) into out/stills; this is how every leaf was checked, 3–10 stills per leaf.
- `bash record.sh [--jobs N]` records all 3900 frames through N parallel headless Edge instances
  (each posts a range of frames), then one `/done` call renders the score to WAV and encodes
  out/apocalypse-of-peter.mp4 (libx264 crf 16 + aac 192k).
- `bash sheet.sh [video] [s]` tiles a contact sheet (one frame every s seconds) for review.
- `npm run srt` (apocalypse/make-srt.js) writes out/apocalypse-of-peter.srt, subtitles that read the
  page's words, generated from the leaves' SPEC objects so they stay in sync; `--plain` writes a
  tag-free variant for text-to-speech. Cues are paced across each leaf's reading window in
  proportion to length (the page inks text fast and holds it), with the litany on its trumpet times.
- The score is validated in Node (render time, peak, RMS per second at the cue landmarks) and
  with `ffmpeg -af volumedetect`; it cannot be listened to in this environment.
- In the browser, main.js wraps each leaf in `safeDraw`, so a leaf that throws paints its error
  on the page instead of blanking the book (useful while leaves are built in parallel).

## 7. Build notes (deviations from the storyboard above)

- Leaf III: seven litany lines did not fit under the verse at 25/22 px, so leaf3.js pre-typesets
  its own spec (verse 24/32, litany 19/24). Text and times unchanged.
- Leaf IV: the drawers are labelled TAX / SANCTION / SURVEIL / LIBEL and the plinth LEDGER /
  TREATY / CODE; 16 eyes on the beast, 7 in the verso margins; the tape that creeps into the
  gutter passes under the panel rule (the rule is drawn after the leaf).
- Leaf V: the aged face arrives at 85.8 so the 86.0 split-flap lands on a visible face.
- Leaf VI: the breadcrumb trail runs from centre toward the door so the crawling seer meets
  crumbs that have already landed; the pope scales down as he steps through the low door.
- Leaf VII: the mirror zoom is ×8 (not ×4.5) so the glass truly fills the panel; the seer is drawn
  mirrored so his front arm sweeps the staff and raises the glass; the panel rule is suppressed
  after 117 via a `noFrame` getter so the flood can cover it.
- Running heads auto-shrink to fit the text block (the Fourth Leaf's head is long).
- The page turn keeps the old verso visible through the first half of the fold; the back of the
  turning leaf is the new verso, which sweeps over it in the second half.
